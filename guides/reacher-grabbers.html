Reacher Grabber Tools: A Simple Buying Guide

A reacher (also called a grabber or pick-up tool) helps people pick things up from the floor or reach high shelves without bending or climbing. It is especially helpful after hip or back surgery, or for anyone with balance problems.

Length

  • About 26 inches: lighter and easier to control. Good for picking up small items while seated.
  • About 32 inches: reaches the floor from standing for most people, and higher shelves.
  • Folding models: handy for car trips and bags.

Longer is not always better: a long reacher can feel heavy at the tip.

Jaw (the end that grips)

  • Rubber cups or pads grip smooth things like cans, bottles, and phones.
  • Wide jaws help with larger items like folded clothes.
  • A small magnet at the tip helps pick up keys and pins.
  • A rotating head lets the user turn the jaws sideways without twisting the wrist.

Trigger and handle

Try to choose a trigger that closes with light pressure. People with arthritis may prefer a full-hand trigger over a two-finger trigger. A handle that locks closed can help carry items without squeezing the whole time.

Weight

Most reachers are light aluminum or plastic. Lighter tools are easier to use for long periods, but very cheap plastic ones can bend.

Get two

Many families buy one for the bedroom and one for the living room or kitchen, so it is always within reach. A clip or hook to hang it near a chair helps too.

Safety reminder

Reachers are for light items. Do not use them for heavy pots or glass items on high shelves. Move heavy, everyday items to waist-height shelves instead.

Bottom line

Choose a length that fits how the person will use it (seated or standing), rubber-grip jaws, and a trigger that is easy to squeeze. Keep one in each room where it will be needed.
