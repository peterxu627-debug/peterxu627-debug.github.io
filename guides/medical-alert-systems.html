Medical Alert Systems: Questions to Ask Before You Sign Up

By Peter Xu · Updated November 2026

A medical alert system lets an older adult call for help by pressing a button, often worn as a pendant or wristband. For people who live alone, it can bring real peace of mind. But plans differ a lot, so it pays to ask questions before signing up.

Home or on-the-go?

  • In-home systems work through a base station and cover the house and sometimes the yard.
  • Mobile systems work through cellular networks and often include GPS, so they work outside the home.

Choose based on how often the person goes out alone.

Monitored or unmonitored?

  • Monitored systems connect to a 24/7 call center that can send help and call family. These usually have a monthly fee.
  • Unmonitored devices call or text family members directly. These may have no monthly fee, but someone must always be ready to answer.

Questions to ask any company

  1. What is the total monthly cost, and what does the first year cost including equipment and activation fees?
  2. Is there a long-term contract or cancellation fee?
  3. Is automatic fall detection available, and what does it cost extra?
  4. Is the button waterproof so it can be worn in the shower?
  5. How long does the battery last, and how is it charged?
  6. What is the range from the base station?
  7. Which cellular network does the mobile device use, and is coverage good where the person lives?
  8. Can the person speak to the operator through the pendant, or only through the base station?
  9. Is there a free trial or money-back period?

About fall detection

Automatic fall detection can call for help if the person cannot press the button. It is helpful but not perfect: it can miss some falls and sometimes trigger by mistake. Encourage the person to press the button whenever they need help.

Wear it

The best system only works if it is worn, including in the bathroom and at night. Choose a style the person is comfortable wearing.

Bottom line

Decide between home and mobile coverage, compare the true yearly cost, avoid long contracts, and pick something the person will actually wear every day.
