Shower Chair or Transfer Bench? How to Choose

By Peter Xu · Updated November 2026

Standing in a wet shower is hard for many older adults, and the bathroom is a common place for falls. A shower seat lets the person sit while washing. The right type depends on the bathroom and on how well the person can step and turn.

The three main types

  • Shower stool: no back, small footprint. Good for people with fair balance in a small shower stall.
  • Shower chair with back: more support, and many have armrests to help with sitting down and standing up. Good for people who tire easily.
  • Tub transfer bench: a long bench with two legs inside the tub and two outside. The person sits down outside the tub, then slides across. This avoids stepping over the tub wall, which is the hardest part for many people.

Measure before buying

  1. Inside width of the tub or shower floor.
  2. Height of the tub wall (for transfer benches).
  3. The seat height the person needs: when seated, knees should be about level with hips and feet flat on the floor.

Most good chairs have legs that adjust in steps. Make sure every leg is set to the same height and locked.

Weight capacity and stability

Check the stated weight limit and choose one with room to spare. Look for rubber tips on all legs, wide-set legs, and a frame that does not wobble. Aluminum frames resist rust.

Seat details

  • Drain holes keep water from pooling.
  • A slightly padded or textured seat is less slippery.
  • A cut-out or "hygienic" seat makes washing easier for some people.

Add the other pieces

A shower seat works best with:

  • A handheld shower head on a hose, so the person can wash while seated.
  • Grab bars installed into studs (not towel bars).
  • A non-slip mat or strips inside the tub.

When to ask a professional

If the person has had falls, has trouble standing up from a chair, or uses a walker, ask their doctor about a home-safety visit from an occupational therapist. They can recommend the exact type and placement.

Bottom line

Use a stool or chair for walk-in showers, and a transfer bench when stepping over a tub wall is risky. Measure first, set the legs evenly, and pair the seat with a handheld shower and grab bars.
