Night Lights and Fall Prevention After Dark

By Peter Xu · Updated November 2026

Getting up at night is a common time for falls: the room is dark, the person may be sleepy or dizzy, and the path to the bathroom may have rugs, cords, or furniture in the way. Good lighting is one of the easiest changes a family can make.

Motion-sensor lights

Lights that turn on when they sense movement mean nobody has to find a switch in the dark. Types include:

  • Plug-in night lights for outlets along the hallway.
  • Battery or rechargeable stick-on lights for places without outlets, such as under the bed frame or along stairs.
  • Under-bed light strips that glow softly when feet touch the floor.

Brightness and color

Choose a warm, soft light that is bright enough to see the floor and edges of furniture, but not so bright that it fully wakes the person or hurts the eyes. Many lights have a low and high setting. Very blue, cold light can be harsh at night.

Where to place them

  1. Beside the bed, low near the floor.
  2. Along the path to the bathroom.
  3. Inside the bathroom, near the toilet.
  4. At the top and bottom of any stairs.
  5. Near the kitchen if the person gets up for water.

Walk the path yourself at night with the room lights off. Any dark patch is a place for another light.

Power: plug-in or battery?

Plug-in lights never need charging but are limited to outlets. Battery lights can go anywhere but need checking. Rechargeable lights with a USB cable are a middle option. Put battery checks on the calendar every month or two.

Other night-time safety steps

  • Remove loose rugs or fix them with non-slip backing.
  • Keep cords along walls.
  • Put a phone and a flashlight within reach of the bed.
  • Encourage sitting on the edge of the bed for a moment before standing, to avoid dizziness.

Bottom line

A few motion-sensor lights along the bed-to-bathroom path cost little and can prevent a serious fall. Walk the route in the dark and light every shadow.
