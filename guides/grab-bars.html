Grab Bars at Home: Suction Cup vs. Screw-Mounted

A grab bar is a simple way to make bathrooms and hallways safer. But not all grab bars are equal, and the difference can matter in a fall.

Screw-mounted grab bars

These attach to the wall with screws into wall studs or with special wall anchors. When installed correctly, they are designed to hold a person's full weight if they slip. This is the type to rely on for support.

What to look for:

  • Stainless steel or coated metal that resists rust.
  • A textured or knurled grip so wet hands do not slide.
  • A length that fits the spot (12, 16, 18 and 24 inches are common).
  • A diameter that fits the hand comfortably, usually around 1.25 to 1.5 inches.

Suction-cup grab bars

These stick to smooth tile or glass without drilling. They are easy to move and good for renters, but they can lose grip over time, especially on textured or grout-lined surfaces. Many makers say they are for balance help only, not for holding full body weight. Read the instructions carefully and test the seal before every use.

Use suction bars as a temporary or travel solution, not as the main support for someone who is unsteady.

Where to put grab bars

  • Next to the toilet, to help with sitting and standing.
  • On the wall inside the tub or shower, where the person enters.
  • On the back wall of the shower, at a comfortable height.
  • In hallways or by steps where the person often reaches for furniture.

A good test: watch where the person already puts their hands. That is often where a bar should go.

Installing

If you are not sure how to find studs or drill through tile, hire a handyman. A grab bar is only as strong as its mounting. Some local agencies on aging offer free or low-cost home-safety installs; it is worth asking.

Don't use towel bars

Towel bars and soap dishes are not built to hold weight. If a person tends to grab them, replace them with real grab bars.

Bottom line

For real support, use screw-mounted bars fixed into studs or proper anchors. Suction bars are for light balance help and short-term use. Place bars where hands naturally reach.
