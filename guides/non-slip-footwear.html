Non-Slip Socks and House Shoes for Safer Steps

Many falls happen inside the home, and footwear plays a part. Walking in socks on smooth floors, or in loose backless slippers, can make slipping and tripping more likely.

Grip socks

Socks with rubber dots or patterns on the sole give more traction than plain socks on wood, tile, or vinyl floors. Look for:

  • Grip on the bottom (and sometimes the top, so they work either way).
  • A comfortable, non-binding cuff, especially for people with swelling or diabetes.
  • The right size; grip dots in the wrong place help less.

Grip socks are good for short walks at home and for sleeping, but they are not a replacement for real shoes for longer walking.

House shoes

A good house shoe:

  • Has a closed back or a heel strap so the foot does not slide out.
  • Has a thin, firm, non-slip sole, not a thick, squishy one that can catch on rugs.
  • Fits snugly without being tight.
  • Uses hook-and-loop straps if tying laces is hard, or if feet swell during the day.

Avoid backless slip-ons, worn-out soles, and very loose or stretched slippers.

Extra-wide and adjustable options

People with swollen feet, bunions, or diabetes may need extra-wide or adjustable shoes. For diabetes, ask the doctor or a podiatrist about proper footwear and daily foot checks.

Replace when worn

Check soles every few months. Smooth, shiny soles have lost their grip.

Bottom line

Choose closed-back house shoes with firm non-slip soles, or grip socks for short trips around the house, and replace them when they wear down.
