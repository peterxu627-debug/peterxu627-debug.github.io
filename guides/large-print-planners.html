Large-Print Calendars and Planners: What Actually Helps

By Peter Xu · Updated November 2026

For many older adults, a paper calendar is the center of the week: doctor visits, birthdays, trash day, when the grandchildren call. When the print gets too small, people stop writing things down, and appointments get missed. A good large-print planner fixes that.

Font size and weight

"Large print" is not a strict standard. Look at sample pages before buying. Dates and day names should be readable at arm's length, and the printed text should be bold or semi-bold, not thin and stylish. Thin gray lines and pale colors look nice in photos but are hard to see.

Contrast

Black text on white or cream paper is the most readable. Avoid busy backgrounds, watercolor washes behind the dates, and colored text on colored paper.

Layout: monthly, weekly, or both

  • Monthly view only: best for people with a few appointments a month. One page shows everything.
  • Weekly view: gives more writing space for each day, useful if the person writes notes, medicine reminders, or phone numbers.
  • Both: many planners combine a month spread with weekly pages. This is helpful but makes the book thicker.

Check how big the daily boxes are. Large handwriting needs boxes at least an inch or two tall.

Paper and binding

  • A planner that lies flat (spiral or wire binding) is easier to write in than a glued paperback.
  • Thicker paper stops ink from showing through.
  • A sturdy cover helps if it lives on the kitchen table.

Useful extra pages

  • Important phone numbers and doctors
  • Medicine list
  • Birthdays and anniversaries
  • Notes pages

Fewer, larger extra pages are better than many tiny boxes.

Wall calendar or desk planner?

A large wall calendar near the kitchen works well for shared households: everyone can see it. A desk planner is more private and portable. Some families use both: the wall calendar for big events, the planner for daily notes.

Tips for caregivers

  • Write appointments in the planner together, so the person knows the system.
  • Use one pen color for medical appointments and another for family events.
  • At the end of each week, look ahead together at the next seven days.

Bottom line

Choose readability over decoration: bold dates, high contrast, big boxes, and a binding that lies flat. Then make writing in it part of a weekly routine.
